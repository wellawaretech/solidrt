# A procedural tile world generates its chunks on the main thread

## Symptom

A tile layer under `retain: "none"` regenerates every chunk through
`fill(chunk)` at its enter, synchronously, in the frame the enter is
delivered: a few hundred microseconds of `setTiles` for a stored map,
but a chunk GENERATED (noise, a cellular automaton, a simulation step)
costs what the generator costs, on the JS thread, in that frame -
Minecraft's main-thread chunk generation hitch. There is no way to
generate a chunk in an isolate and hand it over, because the layer's
handler is its own synchronous enter/leave pair over the gate, not
core's lifecycle (okf/done/2d-region-handlers.md built the sprite
grid on that; okf/plans/3d-world-residency.md said the tile layer
"migrates when the population handler is written").

## Done looks like

- The tile layer is a handler over core's `createRegionGrid`: `fill`
  becomes `load(chunk, signal)` (sync or async; a `"use isolate"`
  generator returns the cells), the upload and bake its build, the
  leave's release its teardown, `retain` the lifecycle's (a count, LRU)
  with "records" and "none" expressed over it.
- The enter's frame still bakes a chunk whose load is synchronous: the
  build is one step, and the slice drain runs ahead of the frame's
  flush, so a chunk shows complete or not at all as today.
- The states (`loading`, `building`, `failed`) and the stats come for
  free; a `fill` that throws is fail-soft.
- tiles-residency.test.tsx gains the async case.
